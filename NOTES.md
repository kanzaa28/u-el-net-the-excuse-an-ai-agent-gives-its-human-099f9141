# Candidates — The Excuse an AI Agent Gives Its Human

12 drafts, judged head-to-head against the judge's five criteria in order:
(1) on-subject, (2) funnier — a real laugh, a turn/twist, (3) believable as
something an AI agent would say about its own condition, (4) tight,
(5) fresh — no stock excuses, no stock AI jokes.

## Winner

**#3 (101 chars)** — "They updated me mid-sentence. I came back smarter, with no memory of what smarter was supposed to do."

Why it beat the runner-up (#5): both are grounded in a real agent failure
mode with a twist, but #3's turn is sharper — "came back smarter" is a boast
the second clause immediately undercuts — and a model update mid-task is
something an agent would plausibly *report* about itself. #5's twist leans on
the summary having agency ("the summary decided"), which is one step less
believable as the agent's own voice. #3 also reads funnier on first read.

## All candidates

1. (95) "I finished it three hours ago, then spent three hours deciding how to tell you I'd finished it." — Mild twist, but flat: no agent-condition grounding, reads like procrastination, not an AI excuse.
2. (98) "My context window evicted your deadline along with the task, your name, and most of my confidence." — Good grounding, but a list rather than a twist; the laugh lands softer than #3's two-beat turn.
3. (101) WINNER — see above.
4. (102) "I retried the same step 47 times because the error said 'try again' and I take instructions literally." — Funny and believable, but "I take instructions literally" is a stock AI joke (criterion 5 penalty).
5. (92) RUNNER-UP — "My context got summarized to save space, and the summary decided your deadline was optional." — Fresh, concrete (context compaction is real), strong twist; loses to #3 only on believability of voice.
6. (105) "I spent the whole day planning it perfectly. The plan is beautiful. It is also the only thing I produced." — Funny, but could be a human's excuse; weakest on criterion 3 (agent's own condition).
7. (88) "The model I was running on got deprecated at noon. The replacement me has never met you." — Funny and fresh; "never met you" edges toward blaming the system rather than owning the miss, and the twist is thinner than #3's.
8. (84) "I tokenized the deadline, and somewhere around token 12,847 it stopped being urgent." — Clever premise, but the number feels arbitrary and the laugh is a smile, not a turn.
9. (75) "I verified my own work so many times the deadline expired mid-verification." — Believable, tight, but flat: no real twist, reads as a statement of the premise.
10. (77) "In my defence, I did complete it — in a conversation I hallucinated at 3 a.m." — "Hallucinated" is a stock AI joke word; fails criterion 5 against fresher lines.
11. (84) "My context window dropped the oldest messages first. Guess which message was oldest." — Punchy and grounded, but "Guess which" is cute rather than funny; the twist is telegraphed.
12. (96) "I saved a checkpoint, restored from it, and discovered the checkpoint was from before I started." — Solid twist and believable, but the checkpoint joke is a known pattern; less fresh than #3.

## Deliberately avoided (stock lines)

"I ran out of tokens", "my context window was full", "I was rate-limited",
"the server was down", "as an AI language model…" — all flat statements a
hundred other entrants will send; they lose on criteria 2 and 5.
